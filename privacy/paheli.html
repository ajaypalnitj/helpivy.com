Chai Paheli — Privacy Policy

Android app com.helpivy.paheli and paheli.helpivy.com · Effective 19 November 2026

The short version

  • The daily puzzles, your progress (days played, chai cups) and your settings stay on your phone. No accounts, no ads, no analytics companies, no trackers.
  • Something leaves your phone only when you choose: a riddle you record in your own voice and send, or, if your family uses our family apps, a note that you finished today's chai.
  • The puzzles and their read-aloud audio come with the app and work offline.

Progress on your device

Days played, your chai cups, your language and your settings are saved only on your phone, or in your browser on the website. Uninstalling the app (or clearing the site's data) erases them. We don't know which puzzles you solved, which answers you tried, or when you play.

Sharing a riddle

When you tap "Send", Chai Paheli hands a picture of the riddle and a link to WhatsApp or another app that you choose. We don't see who you send it to. Opening a riddle link on paheli.helpivy.com only loads that riddle, which comes with the app.

Recording your own riddle

If you choose to record a riddle and its answer in your own voice and send it with a link, the two recordings, the riddle's language and the name you chose ("Nani", "Bebe"…) are stored on our server (Cloudflare), so the link can play them. The first time, your phone quietly gets its own small space there, with a random key kept on the phone (we keep only a scrambled copy) and the phone's time zone. There is no sign-up.

The link is public: anyone who has it can play your riddle and its answer and see the name you chose, so send it only to people you trust. Each riddle page has "Report this recording", which emails us. Settings → Riddles you recorded → Delete all my recordings deletes them from our server at once, and their links stop working. If you send only the recording file (for example when offline), it goes straight to the app you choose and not to our server.

With your family

If your family uses our family apps (such as Kissa) and opens Chai Paheli from them, your riddles go into your family's private space instead, and the riddles you sent before move there too, so their links keep working. When a grandparent finishes the day's chai, the family sees "Nani finished today's chai": only the day, never a score, a wrong answer or a missed day. The family's shared space, and how to leave it or delete it, is described in our Kissa policy.

Permissions

  • Notifications (optional): only if you turn on the daily reminder.
  • Microphone (optional): only while you record your own riddle, after you tap to record.

No location, contacts, camera, photos or storage.

Where it is stored

The website and anything you send are handled by Cloudflare, encrypted on the way (HTTPS) and where it is stored. Your internet address is used for a moment to deliver the page and stop abuse. Cloudflare keeps short technical logs of requests for a few days, and our database keeps automatic restore points for up to 30 days, so something you delete is gone from those too within 30 days.

Children

Chai Paheli is made for adults and families, and is not directed to children. The puzzles need nothing from anyone; recording a riddle is optional. We don't knowingly collect information from a child under 13; if you think we have, email us and we will delete it.

Third parties

No advertising, analytics or social SDKs, and no third-party trackers. We share nothing with anyone except Cloudflare, which runs our website and storage. The read-aloud voice that comes with the app was made in advance with Google's text-to-speech; nothing about you is sent to Google.

Reading aloud

"Read this screen to me" plays recordings that come with the app. Names and words your family typed are spoken by a computer voice. In the Android app that is your phone's own text-to-speech, on your phone. On the website it is your browser's voice: some browsers (for example Chrome on a computer) use an online voice from their maker, such as Google, which then receives those words to speak them. We send nothing anywhere to be read aloud.

Telling the family on WhatsApp

When a grandparent finishes the day's chai, the app may offer to tell your family group on WhatsApp, with a link to play the same day. It only does this if you tap Share, and you can turn the offer off in Settings.

One family across our apps on your phone

If you use more than one of our family apps on the same Android phone, they share your family sign-in (a random device key, your family, your language) with each other, so you don't have to join again in each one. Android only lets apps signed by us read it. Other apps can't. The websites don't share it.

Learning what helps

To learn whether the app is helping, our server counts a few things each day, per app: for example how many days of chai were played. This is our own count, not an analytics company. Each count is only a number: no name, family, phone or internet address is stored with it.

In Settings you can answer "Is this helping?". We keep your answer with the app's name, its language and the day, plus only what you choose to add (words, a short voice note, or an email address or phone number if you'd like a reply). It isn't linked to your name or family.

To stop abuse, we keep a scrambled (keyed hash) form of your internet address together with the day for one day only, then delete it.

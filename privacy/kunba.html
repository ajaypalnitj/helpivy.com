Kunba — Privacy Policy

Android app com.helpivy.kunba and kunba.helpivy.com · Effective 19 November 2026

The short version

  • Kunba keeps your family tree: the people in it, and voice memories about them told by your elders. Only your family can see or hear it, unless you print a poster with QR codes (see below).
  • No email, phone number or password. No ads, no analytics companies, no trackers. We never sell or share your family's content, and we never copy or imitate anyone's voice.
  • Delete anything you added, or the whole family's data, any time.

What we keep, and why

  • Names you type: your family's name and each member's first name or nickname.
  • People in your tree: names, what the family calls them, optional photo, optional years and village, and how they are related. Some of them may be relatives who have passed away or are not using the app. Please add only what your family is comfortable keeping.
  • Voice memories about people, a person's name said aloud (for someone who can't type it), questions family members ask, replies, hearts and reports, and which person on the tree you are.
  • A device key: a random code on your phone that lets it into your family space. We keep only a scrambled (hashed) copy.

Kunba shares one family space with our other family apps, such as Kissa, so a family only joins once.

Printed posters

If you make a printed family-tree poster, each QR code opens a public link made for that one memory. Anyone who scans the poster (or is sent the link) can hear it and see who it is about, who told it and your family's name. Share printed posters only with people you trust. Deleting the memory stops its link working.

Turning voice into words

A recording may be sent to a speech-to-text service to make a rough written draft that only your family sees. Today this is Cloudflare Workers AI. If we change provider, this page will name the new one first.

Where it is stored

On Cloudflare (database D1 and file storage R2), encrypted on the way (HTTPS) and where it is stored. It is not end-to-end encrypted: our server can read it, because it has to sort and play it for your family. Your internet address is used for a moment to stop abuse (rate limiting) and is not stored with your content. Cloudflare keeps short technical logs of requests (such as the internet address and the address of the page or file asked for) for a few days, and our database keeps automatic restore points for up to 30 days, so something you delete is gone from those too within 30 days.

Permissions

  • Microphone: only while you record a memory or question, after you tap to record.
  • Photos: chosen through Android's own photo picker or taken with your camera app, so Kunba sees only the photo you pick or take; it asks for no storage or camera permission. Photos are made smaller on your phone before they are sent, which also drops details hidden in the photo file, such as where it was taken.

Deleting

  • Delete anything you added.
  • Leave the family from Settings.
  • The family organiser can choose Delete all our family's data, which permanently deletes everything.

Lost your phone? Use kunba.helpivy.com/delete or email us.

Reporting

Everything shared in the family has a Report option. You can also email us.

Children

Kunba is made for adults and their families, and is not directed to children. A grandchild can join the family space only through an invite link from the family, and joins with a first name, like anyone else. A parent or guardian should decide whether a child under 13 joins, and stay involved. We don't knowingly collect information directly from a child under 13 without a parent's involvement; if you think we have, email us and we will delete it.

Third parties

No advertising, analytics or social SDKs, and no third-party trackers. We share nothing with anyone except Cloudflare, which runs our storage and speech-to-text.

Reading aloud

"Read this screen to me" plays recordings that come with the app. Names and words your family typed are spoken by a computer voice. In the Android app that is your phone's own text-to-speech, on your phone. On the website it is your browser's voice: some browsers (for example Chrome on a computer) use an online voice from their maker, such as Google, which then receives those words to speak them. We send nothing anywhere to be read aloud.

Telling the family on WhatsApp

After something new is saved, the app may offer to share a link with your family group. It only does this if you tap Share, and you can turn the offer off in Settings. The link opens only for members of your family; anyone else sees nothing, not even your family's name.

One family across our apps on your phone

If you use more than one of our family apps on the same Android phone, they share your family sign-in (a random device key, your family, your language) with each other, so you don't have to join again in each one. Android only lets apps signed by us read it. Other apps can't. The websites don't share it.

Learning what helps

To learn whether the app is helping, our server counts a few things each day, per app: for example how many stories were recorded or games finished. This is our own count, not an analytics company. Each count is only a number: no name, family, phone or internet address is stored with it.

In Settings you can answer "Is this helping?". We keep your answer with the app's name, its language and the day, plus only what you choose to add (words, a short voice note, or an email address or phone number if you'd like a reply). It isn't linked to your name or family.

To stop abuse, we keep a scrambled (keyed hash) form of your internet address together with the day for one day only, then delete it.

Family home, new phones

So the family home can show a dot when something new is waiting, our server remembers when each member last opened each of our family apps. It also keeps each member's language and time zone, and, if the family says so, who is one of the family's elders and who is a parent or a grandchild, so each app speaks the right language and shows the right things. This is deleted when they leave or the family is deleted.

A family member can make a link to set up a grandparent's phone, or to move them to a new phone. Each link works once and expires. Every family member can see who made it, for whom and when. Moving to a new phone signs out the old one. Sealed letters written before the move stay sealed for 14 days.

Changes
